# Research Summary

## Paper

**Digitalization and Entrepreneurial Engagement in Developing Economies Based on Evidence from Pakistan**

Faisal Umar, Iman Muhammad Asif, Mehar Tahir Farid. 2026 20th International Conference on Ubiquitous Information Management and Communication (IMCOM), IEEE, 2026.

## Research question

The study examines how access to three digital technologies — mobile phones, personal computers and the internet — is associated with entrepreneurial engagement in Pakistan's non-agricultural sector. It also examines differences by gender and rural/urban location.

## Data

The study uses the Pakistan Social and Living Standards Measurement (PSLM) Survey 2020–2021 made available by the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics. The final analytical sample reported in the paper contains 192,670 individuals in non-agricultural economic activity.

The repository does not include the survey microdata.

## Method

Entrepreneurship is treated as a binary outcome. The paper uses binary logistic regression with digital-access variables and controls for province, region, gender, age, education and household income. The results are reported primarily as odds ratios.

## Main findings

The paper reports three distinct patterns:

- mobile access has a strong positive association with entrepreneurship
- computer access has a negative association with entrepreneurship
- internet access has a smaller positive association

The study also reports substantial heterogeneity across rural/urban and male/female subgroups.

## Interpretation

The paper argues that digital inclusion policies should not treat all technologies as interchangeable. Mobile connectivity appears particularly relevant in resource-constrained settings, while internet access provides a more modest positive association. The negative computer association is interpreted cautiously and is discussed in relation to formal employment, education and administrative use.

These are associations from cross-sectional survey data. They should not be interpreted as proof of causal effects.
